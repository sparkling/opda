Hello Alex Morgan,

Your participation in the Conveyancing Working Group has been approved. This invitation is for this group only. If you selected other groups, each is reviewed separately and you will receive a separate invitation for each one that is approved.

OPDA is bringing the property sector together to make UK property transaction data interoperable, verifiable and consent-driven. Today, the same information is repeatedly re-entered, translated and interpreted as it moves between organisations and systems. The OPDA Smart Property Data Standard will establish a shared language so that information can be understood, exchanged and reused with greater confidence.

Rather than forcing the whole property lifecycle into one universal model, OPDA is creating a governed family of connected domain models, each shaped by the people who understand that part of the market. Conveyancing is one part of that wider standard. The models will preserve precise local meaning while allowing information to cross boundaries, and will support familiar outputs such as business glossaries, data dictionaries, schemas, APIs, forms and readable documentation.

Your experience can help distinguish the property, its legal title, the parties to a transaction and the evidence used in legal due diligence. Help us explain enquiries, obligations and transaction milestones without losing qualifications or treating every transaction as the same case.

You are not being asked to understand ontologies, adopt AI, or have technical knowledge. We ask you to share the material your organisation already uses, contribute your domain knowledge in Teams and challenge successive model drafts. OPDA will use an AI-assisted, human-governed process to turn that evidence and feedback into reviewable candidates, publishing and revising them with the group until the Conveyancing model is ready to become its first official working-group draft.

📤 How to contribute source material

Your private company folder is ready. It is separate from the files shared with the whole working group in Teams.

  1. Open your company folder. Use your organisation’s Source Material Intake folder. This link opens the private organisation area you are permitted to access for this working group.
  2. Organise your area as you need. You may upload files and create, edit, rename, move or delete folders, documents and notes in your organisation’s area.
  3. Keep a README at the top level. Include an index and enough explanation for OPDA to understand the contents, their provenance, version and any usage restrictions. Keep it current as the material changes.

SharePoint uploads require an approved company-domain account. This does not affect participation in Teams. If your organisation needs another company account added for uploads, email smartdata@openpropdata.org.uk.

Ordinary Teams files are shared with the working group. They are not the private company-folder intake route. Do not upload private source material to a channel’s shared files.

💬 How to participate

Reply in threads. With many participants, threads keep each subject together and make the discussion manageable.

Open the most relevant channel and check whether a thread already covers the subject. If it does, select Reply beneath the original post. The Start a post box creates a separate thread, so use it only for a genuinely new subject.

🔐 Access and privacy

  • Invitation only, with approval for each group. Access to this working group does not approve you for another. To invite additional colleagues, email smartdata@openpropdata.org.uk with their names, email addresses and organisations.
  • Private organisation area. Where private company-folder access has been granted, approved company-domain contributors can view, create, upload, edit, rename, move and delete material in their organisation’s area. Other organisations cannot see it.
  • Permissions stay controlled. Contributors cannot view another organisation’s submissions, invite people, change permissions or make material public.

📚 Useful source material

For this domain, useful starting points include blank property-information and enquiry forms, title-checking guidance, search-result structures, transaction checklists, completion requirements and definitions of legal process statuses. Explain jurisdiction, scope and exceptions. Do not submit client files, live title documents or confidential correspondence.

  • Schemas and APIs: JSON Schemas; OpenAPI, Swagger and AsyncAPI definitions; GraphQL schemas; XML Schemas.
  • Database structures: database schemas, entity-relationship diagrams, data dictionaries and non-production DDL extracts.
  • Data and field lists: CSV, TSV, spreadsheets, JSON and XML.
  • Documents, guidance and blank forms: PDF, Word, Markdown and plain text.
  • Taxonomies, controlled vocabularies and semantic models: CSV, spreadsheets, Turtle/RDF, OWL and JSON-LD.
  • Diagrams and screenshots: draw.io, Visio, SVG, PNG, JPEG and PDF.
Audio and video files are not accepted. Recordings must be transcribed before submission. Include the transcript’s date, speakers and context where known.
Upload the material itself; do not submit links. OPDA will not follow links to websites, cloud drives, shared folders or online documents. If authorised, download or export the material and place the file in your private company folder.

⛔ Do not submit

  • Personal, customer or live property-transaction data.
  • Passwords, API keys, credentials, private keys or other secrets.
  • Material restricted by confidentiality, contract, licence or NDA.
  • Executable software, active scripts or macros.
  • Password-protected files, encrypted archives or compressed archives.

What happens next

OPDA keeps the original source, reviews it for security and suitability, and uses approved information as evidence for AI-assisted data-model candidates. People review every candidate before it becomes a working-group draft. Source files are not automatically published or shared with other participants.
