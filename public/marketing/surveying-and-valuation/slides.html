Speaker notes

Introduce OPDA using its full name. Explain that this is a programme in development and an invitation to contribute professional experience. This five-minute introduction is not a statement of adopted requirements.

OPDA working groups · 02

A problem in everyday work

An inspection or valuation produces findings that others rely on. Observations, professional judgements and limitations need to remain distinguishable when those findings are shared.

Open Property Data Association2 / 6
Speaker notes

Ask whether this is familiar to the audience. Example: Two floor-area figures can differ because they include different spaces, even when both measurements are accurate. These are illustrative situations, not evidence about a particular organisation.

OPDA working groups · 03

The work in development

Working groups are developing shared definitions for property information. The aim is to make meaning clearer when people and organisations exchange information.

Open Property Data Association3 / 6
Speaker notes

Explain shared definitions in ordinary language. The work develops a specification and recommendations. OPDA is not asking participants to operate a linked-data application.

OPDA working groups · 04

The contribution you can make

  • Explain the distinctions that matter in your work.
  • Bring authorised examples and difficult cases.
  • Review draft definitions and challenge assumptions.
Open Property Data Association4 / 6
Speaker notes

Help explain how measurements and findings should carry their basis, qualifications and uncertainty beyond the original report.

OPDA working groups · 05

Your professional judgement matters

You do not need data-modelling expertise. Your professional experience and judgement can help shape the work.

Open Property Data Association5 / 6
Speaker notes

Welcome experience from small organisations and public-interest representatives as well as large firms. Do not invent meeting schedules, time commitments, accreditation or endorsement.
