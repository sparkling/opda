# Launch industry sessions through reviewed trade-body outreach

## Context and Problem Statement

Maria's 1 October Teams messages asked Henrik and Paul to set the order and dates
for industry introductions; she offered trade-body/regulator emails and LinkedIn
support. On 9 October she asked where the sessions stood and whether she needed to
do anything. The 5 October call and Paul's action email identified the intended
route: trusted organisations distribute member-ready material, supported by
personal approaches from Paul and Maria and infrastructure/templates from Henrik.

The call discussed possible endorsement and co-branding. It did not establish
that any organisation had agreed, that logo use was authorised, or that campaign
messages had been sent. The existing Marketing toolkit and campaign decisions
already own the assets and signup proposition; this record must not create another
copy of those materials or an independent sending system.

## Considered Options

- Send a single broad OPDA broadcast to a newly assembled contact population.
- Ask every trade body to create its own invitation from scratch.
- Use personal introductions and reviewed, reusable member-ready packs distributed by each body.

## Decision Outcome

Propose the third option as the operational next step under ADR-0071/0086. Henrik
provides Paul the existing toolkit link, checks the templates with him, and prepares
the introduction-session proposition and materials. Henrik and Paul agree a proposed
domain order, dates, facilitators and what participants are being invited to do.
Paul and Maria lead the named personal approaches and ask organisations to distribute
approved material through their own channels, without supplying their member lists.

Record organisation/contact ownership and response in the existing outreach records.
Endorsement or co-branding may be requested; apply it only after explicit approval.
Reuse canonical signup links, approved participation language and governed rich HTML
emails, with portable social images and post copy from the existing toolkit.

Check intended recipients can reach the signup destination before treating a pack
as launch-ready. Asset preparation is distinct from authorising external email,
LinkedIn publication or session invitations. Creating this ADR sends nothing and
does not change the website's existing access policy or launch the campaign.

### Consequences

- Good, because familiar organisations can reach practitioners without transferring member databases.
- Good, because existing toolkit ownership prevents inconsistent invitation copies.
- Bad, because launch timing depends on contact responses and approval of the material.
- Neutral, because a contact list and prepared pack are not evidence of endorsement or dispatch.

### Confirmation

The research establishes the intended route and assigned preparatory work. It does
not establish current toolkit quality, approved session dates, successful outreach,
public signup reachability or permission to use any trade body's mark.

| Owner | Outstanding action | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Henrik | Provide the existing toolkit to Paul and incorporate his review. | Reviewed asset versions and an accessible canonical signup journey. |
| Henrik + Paul | Agree proposed order, dates and facilitation for industry introductions. | Session plan ready for the relevant organisers to confirm. |
| Paul + Maria | Make personal approaches and agree distribution with named organisations. | Recorded responses, distribution approvals and any branding permission. |
| Henrik | Tell Maria what support is needed and which sessions are confirmed versus proposed. | Clear status and specific requests in the catch-up update. |
