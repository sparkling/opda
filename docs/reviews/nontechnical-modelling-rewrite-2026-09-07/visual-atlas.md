# Visual atlas and two prototype spreads

Design briefs for the proposed [curriculum](curriculum.md). No images, diagrams or website components were generated in this rerun. The purpose is to specify what each future visual should explain, how it will be read and what would make it misleading.

> **Current production note, 10 September 2026:** this dated proposal now follows
> the subject-led illustration rule in `DESIGN.md`. Watercolour is not the default;
> every placement needs a unique concept and composition, and no existing or new
> image may be reused on another page, card or section.

## Editorial direction

An illustrated working field guide: recognisable property work, readable specimens of the agreement being built, then precise diagrams and worked questions. It should be warmer and more explanatory than the ontology reference without implying that domain judgement is simplistic.

Use seven composition families selectively:

| Family | Useful relationship | Essential non-visual equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Situation and question | A professional decision in its setting | The stated facts and question in ordinary prose |
| Annotated specimen | How a definition, source or candidate page is read | Real HTML fields and an ordered explanation |
| Paired comparison | Exactly what differs and why it matters | A comparison table or paired statements |
| Small relationship map | Subjects, direction, setting and connection | Every relevant edge as a complete sentence |
| Multilane timeline | Different kinds of time without false sequencing | Dates/periods labelled by their meaning, including unknowns |
| Evidence trace | Which passage or derivation supports which claim | Source, version, passage, claim and limitation |
| Worked response | How a question changes a proposal | The question, reasoned response and changed/unchanged meaning |

These are composition options, not seven components to build before any teaching. Prototype only what the first chapters need. Use native HTML for specimens and tables; do not rasterise readable content. Diagram production may share the technical section's master/export pipeline, but beginner diagrams need their own simpler reading conventions.

## Professional illustration briefs

Preserve six different professional tasks. They may be six dispersed illustrations or three paired comparisons, each with its own light/dark counterpart. File count is a production choice; professional differentiation is not. Avoid a row of six pictures beside six context names, which would visually assert the equation the prose rejects.

| Perspective | Scene and distinctive objects | Modelling question; likely location |
|---|---|---|
| Estate agency | A listing sheet, property frontage and separately arranged listing stages; no readable UI text | Does a state describe the listing, transaction or dwelling? U4/E2. |
| Finance and banking | Two explicitly different arrangement documents, participant tokens and a modest residential model; no decorative currency pile | In which arrangement is someone borrower or guarantor? E2. |
| Surveying and valuation | A survey desk, two floor-plan overlays, measuring tool and an enclosed-area detail | What does the measured area include? E3. |
| Conveyancing | Two document bundles, their separate dated-event markers and a boundary/title drawing; no fabricated legal seal | Are these uses of “completion” describing the same event? E7. |
| Property data services | Distinct source documents and versions under a comparison lens, with a clear trace back to passages | Which source version supports this statement? E5/C6. |
| Property technology | Two different readable representations of the same situation, with relationship tokens preserved between them | Does the changed representation preserve meaning? U1/C7. |

Choose the technique for each lesson: editorial watercolour/gouache for a humane situation, architectural cutaway or ink-and-coloured-pencil for a physical distinction, short sequential ink narrative for change over time, risograph/screenprint for a declarative opener, or paper craft for a material metaphor. Keep the governed OPDA gold, petrol, violet, rose and coral palette across forms. Light uses paper `#F9F9F9`; dark uses deep-purple paper and pale linework. Approve the light composition, then edit that exact image into dark mode without changing geometry. Never invert it with CSS or mix forms as a random decorative collage.

Campaign art has an existing 4:1, 1200×300 WebP contract below 150KB. Use that for narrow professional strips. A wider narrative composition may use the established learning-illustration proportions, but record its own intentional geometry and preserve it responsively; do not force campaign-card dimensions or an arbitrary fixed height on every image. Commission a unique concept and composition for every placement; existing assets may inform palette and quality but must never be reused, traced or recombined.

No embedded labels, ontology arrows, approval stamps, brand imitations or invented evidence. Exact relationships belong in authored diagrams and HTML. Keep alt text focused on a useful contribution; decorative art should not repeat the entire adjacent explanation. Record the prompt, model/provider, source asset, edit lineage, rights/standing, dimensions and alt/decorative decision when assets are later made.

Reject the market-stalls/common-noticeboard metaphor as the default context illustration: it adds a second analogy and can imply that a shared board resolves semantic differences. Concrete professional questions have stronger relevance here. Reject six variations of hands sorting coloured cards.

## Prototype A · One agreement, six useful views

Target: U2, “What we are building”. This tests whether a non-ontologist can see what they are being invited to review, rather than merely learn the names of outputs.

Opening copy direction:

> We are agreeing what information means: which things it describes, how they connect, which words people use and what conditions matter. You can inspect that agreement through definitions, data descriptions, agreed choices and diagrams. You do not need to write ontology code to say that a definition is wrong or that an important distinction is missing.

Mark the following set once as a fictional teaching package, not candidate content. Its specimens must stand alone without borrowing candidate identifiers or copying a live model record. The ordinary words “Inspection” and “describes” are examples, not claims that a particular source record is adopted.

### Spread 1: meaning and data description

**Glossary specimen: Inspection.** Show a short definition, purpose, scope, ordinary example and counterexample. The counterexample is revising wording in a report without conducting another examination. Use a carefully stated fictional scope; do not present it as a universal professional definition.

**Dictionary specimen: Report issue date.** Show what date is described, which report version it concerns and what it must not be confused with. Include a value and source in a separately labelled teaching record. The issue date is not automatically the inspection date, recording date or applicability period.

A short note connects the two: the glossary explains an idea; the dictionary explains a data element used to describe the situation. Neither replaces the other.

### Spread 2: organising topics and recording choices

**Topic taxonomy specimen.** “Roof condition” is a narrower inspection topic beneath “Building fabric”. Describe a topic relation. Do not draw a roof contained inside a physical building and then label that relation “broader”.

**Controlled vocabulary specimen.** For the explicitly invented scope “physical access to a nonempty, identified set of agreed inspection areas during this visit”, offer three defined choices: access to all agreed areas; access to some but not all agreed areas; access to none of the agreed areas. State the universe and definitions so the choices can be assessed; excluding an empty scope keeps “all” and “none” distinct. Missing evidence is not automatically a fourth outcome or the same as access to none.

This deliberately avoids the overlapping alternatives “completed within scope” and “limited by access”. Physical inspection access is also distinct from permission to receive data in E9.

### Spread 3: resource and relationship definitions

**Resource definition specimen: Inspection.** Explain the kind of identifiable event being described and the identity question. Below it, separately label the particular Harbour Court inspection used in the story. Do not use the instance as the definition.

**Relationship definition specimen: describes.** Explain the intended connection from a report version to an inspection, including its scope. Below it show the particular sentence “Report version 2 describes the 12 August inspection”. A four-node illustrative map can connect two report versions, the inspection and Flat 1; every edge must have an ordinary verb and a full adjacent sentence equivalent.

Explain that six outputs are aligned parts of one semantic package. The formal ontology and generated delivery artefacts serve different readers. One brief sentence about generated JSON-LD is sufficient.

### Practice and a change comparison

First ask six ordinary questions: where would you check a term's meaning, a date's subject, a broader topic, the allowed access outcomes, the identity of a kind of event, or what “describes” connects? Provide the reasoned response visibly.

Then distinguish a label-only edit from a definition change. A proposed alternative label for “report issue date” does not by itself change which date it means. A proposal to make the element represent the inspection's occurrence date changes meaning and must be evaluated against the dictionary, examples, questions and affected relationships or requirements. Taxonomy entries need not change if their meaning is unaffected.

Do not confuse either edit with changing an example instance to include Flat 2. A change to one example's scope is not automatically a change to the model's definition. C7 owns the deeper, version-aware cross-view comparison.

### Layout and failure criteria

Read down three paired spreads with ample heading rhythm, not a six-card wall. Pairs may sit side by side only when both remain readable; stack them on narrow content tracks. Keep specimens in HTML. A small overview can orient readers but cannot substitute for the specimens.

Reject the prototype if it still reads as six labels without examples; if class and instance merge; if a closed list's scope is unstated; or if seeing the agreement requires a technical code block. Do not reject it merely because the two sides of a spread need different specimen structures.

## Prototype B · Two floor areas: what are they measuring?

Target: E3, “Measurements, amounts and values”. This introduces substantial content not already repeated across the current date-correction lessons.

Opening copy direction:

> Two accounts give areas of 68 m² and 72 m² for the same fictional dwelling. Before choosing one, look at what each measures. In this example, one account excludes an enclosed area of 4 m² and the other includes it. The difference is explained by the stated inclusion boundaries—not by deciding that one number looks more authoritative.

Use a landscape survey-desk illustration only if it supports recognition. Below it, two authored, identically oriented schematic plans show the same main outline and the same enclosed area. The first excludes that area; the second includes it. Label the drawings “schematic, not to scale”. Use hatching and text as well as colour.

The information needed to reach the first answer is supplied explicitly:

| Description | Account A | Account B |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | The same stipulated fictional dwelling | The same stipulated fictional dwelling |
| Value and unit | 68 m² | 72 m² |
| Stated inclusion basis | Main area; enclosed 4 m² area excluded | Main area and enclosed 4 m² area included |
| Source | Fictional account A | Fictional account B |
| Measuring standard | None claimed by this teaching example | None claimed by this teaching example |

The first conclusion is determinate within the premises: the stated 4 m² inclusion difference explains the values. It does not select the legally or professionally appropriate basis for a real use. Explain how subject, quantity, unit, basis, method and source become reviewable distinctions.

Then change the case. Two accounts claim to use the same basis, but the method or relevant dates are not supplied. The earlier explanation no longer resolves the discrepancy. Ask what evidence is missing and whether the accounts can yet be treated as conflicting. Preserve the distinction between a supported conclusion and an underdetermined variation.

Add a compact amount comparison: an asking-price statement, an agreed transaction-price statement and a valuation opinion. State their different purpose and maker in the fictional premises. Equal currency and a common dwelling do not make them interchangeable. Keep financial advice and valuation practice outside the lesson.

End with a worked contribution: identify the subject and accounts, state the apparent discrepancy, point to the inclusion evidence and ask for the missing basis or method needed for the changed case. Do not ask the learner to pick a data type or ontology predicate.

On narrow tracks, show account A then B with the comparison table in meaningful document order. The print version uses black outlines and hatching. The text and table carry the entire reasoning if the art or diagram is unavailable. Reject the composition if labels shrink to fit, if the two outlines imply different subjects, or if the example depends on an unintroduced professional standard.

## Responsive and accessible production contract

- Preserve the shared shell, typography, navigation, focus and semantic colour contracts. Authored prose has the existing 1024px maximum measure and left-aligned block; paragraph justification retains a left-aligned last line. Diagrams and tables can use an appropriate wider track.
- Most learning diagrams should fit a left-aligned 960px maximum without forcing every canvas to 960×600. Size the canvas to the actual explanation. Do not place a full graph in a half-column comparison and rely on shrinking it.
- Aim for ordinary diagrams of three to six subjects with sentence-case labels. Split a crowded explanation progressively; a node-count limit is not a semantic test. Keep essential rendered labels at least 16px, not merely 16 source-SVG units.
- Reflow based on the available content track, not only the viewport. Stack comparisons, retain reading order and expose the complete HTML equivalent at every width. No pan/zoom or hover prerequisite for meaning.
- Light/dark variants preserve geometry and wording. Diagram colours come from semantic tokens; colour is never the sole distinction. Print and forced colours retain the explanation, relationships and evidence.
- Keep worked answers visible. Optional local emphasis may enhance a diagram but must not hide the lesson when JavaScript is absent. No scored tests, progress accounts, drag-only actions or LMS machinery.
- Use the existing shared callout component and semantic themes only when the content is genuinely a callout. Do not make every paragraph a callout or every practice step a panel. Ordinary subheadings and prose should do most of the work.
- No repeated chapter-end route lists: the shared navigation already supplies them. Teach with varied compositions, not duplicated navigation cards.
