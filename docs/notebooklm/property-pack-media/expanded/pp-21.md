# Privacy, consent and access in a shared property-data ecosystem

For data stewards, scheme participants, service designers and implementers.

Property information can concern a building, a legal interest, a household or a person's financial circumstances. Some information is useful to many participants; some should reach only particular recipients for a defined purpose. A common data model makes these distinctions expressible, but it does not decide the legal basis for processing or grant access to a system.

The retained materials describe three connected responsibilities. Classification explains the nature and context of data. Policy expresses conditions on its use. Operational controls decide and enforce what happens in a running service. Keeping these responsibilities connected and distinguishable is the central lesson of the accepted OPDA privacy method and the retained draft Smart Data guidebook.

## Start with the purpose and the people affected

The June 2026 draft of the DBT Smart Data guidebook's chapter4 frames stewardship around purposes, users' rights and participants' obligations, rather than a simple claim that one party “owns the data”. It discusses collection, use, retention, deletion, portability, onward sharing and derived information. It is a draft design resource, with suggested clauses expressly subject to consultation, not an adopted property rulebook or a statement of the law currently applicable to a particular transaction.

Consider an illustrative inspection appointment. A service may need a contact name and telephone number to arrange access. Another participant may need the inspection result without needing the occupier's contact details. Sharing the same complete record with everyone would confuse two purposes. Separating the inspection evidence from the contact information makes it possible to design different access and retention arrangements.

The example does not prescribe a retention period or legal basis. Those require qualified review in the actual context. It shows why the ontology should describe the relevant subjects and purposes precisely enough for a responsible person to make that decision.

## Classification describes; it does not authorise

ODR0054 adopts a bounded approach using reviewed mappings to the Data Privacy Vocabulary, or DPV. Its initial areas include sensitivity, personal-data category, applicable regulation, processing purpose, lawful basis, residency or transfer and access level. These are distinct classification schemes. A purpose, a jurisdiction and a transfer basis cannot be treated as interchangeable values because one form happens to collect them together.

The method also keeps sensitivity separate from whether information is master, reference or transactional data. A reusable reference dataset can still contain sensitive material; a transaction record is not automatically personal in every statement. Classification must follow the content and context rather than a broad technical label.

Qualified classifications can identify their subject, level, time and responsible actor. This matters when interpretation changes or differs between contexts. Attaching a “personal” label can support discovery and review, but it cannot prove that a service has implemented appropriate permissions or that a downstream recipient is entitled to receive the information.

## User permission, legal basis and application access

Several meanings of “consent” or “permission” can appear in the same journey. A person may indicate a preference through an interface. An organisation must establish the applicable basis and conditions for its processing. An application may hold technical permission to call an API. These are related facts with different subjects and evidence.

The draft guidebook discusses consent controls and also the need to identify a lawful basis. Its placeholder clauses must not be read as a universal determination that every form of data processing uses consent or that one interface action resolves all obligations. The local modelling decisions deliberately leave the legal judgement to people with the relevant context.

Similarly, an authenticated application connection does not establish that every available field should be returned for every purpose. The operational design must connect participant identity, the request, the applicable rules and the data actually supplied. A record describing those rules can support this work without performing it.

## When a policy language adds value

ODR0057 conditionally adopts ODRL through the DPV profile for three bounded uses: retention conditions, permissions depending on changing conditions such as consent state or geography, and cross-border transfer conditions. DPV describes the target's privacy context; ODRL expresses the policy's permissions, prohibitions, duties and constraints.

This is deliberately narrower than adopting a universal policy engine. The decision requires a suitably DPV-typed target. Merely finding a target link does not establish that the target has the required classification. Its validation obligations therefore test the boundary as well as the presence of a policy object.

The decision also requires human-curated policies. Automated extraction and enrichment must not generate policy, permission, prohibition or duty instances. Extracting a duration from a document does not establish that it is the correct retention period for a particular record. A model's confidence in reading the number cannot supply regulatory intent or approval.

## Expression and enforcement

An illustrative reviewed policy might prohibit further use of inspection-contact details after a defined condition. Publishing that policy as linked data does not delete a database row, revoke a token or prevent a download. A separately implemented service must connect the validated policy to those actions and establish the evidence needed to determine whether the condition holds.

This separation helps allocate responsibility. A data steward may maintain classification. A qualified policy owner may decide a rule. An operating service enforces it. Monitoring and incident handling establish whether the intended behaviour actually occurred. Collapsing those responsibilities into an “access-controlled” label would make failures difficult to diagnose.

The OPDA records are explicit about their maturity. ODR0054 is an accepted method with implementation pending. ODR0057 is conditionally accepted and requires local evidence before an implementation or conformity claim. Neither says that the documentation site enforces data-access policies.

## Risks created by combining useful information

The draft guidebook discusses cross-sector linkage, inference and cumulative exposure. A dataset that seems modest in isolation may reveal more when linked to other data. Property, energy and finance services can therefore create new uses and new risks through combination. The relevant assessment concerns the use case and affected people, as well as individual fields.

It also discusses vulnerability, understandable journeys and keeping accountability traceable when information is passed onward. These concerns connect privacy to service quality. A consumer should be able to understand a meaningful request without becoming responsible for assessing the trustworthiness of every technical participant.

For the Property Pack programme, the useful direction is specific and practical: identify the information and its purpose; retain who classified it and when; obtain qualified decisions about conditions; and connect those decisions to demonstrable operational controls. That provides room for reuse while keeping the effects and limits of each layer visible.
