# Standards maturity, review and a recorded determination

For reviewers, decision makers and working-group chairs.

A model can be generated, published, reviewed, technically validated and adopted at different times. These events answer different questions. Generation produces an artefact. Publication makes a version available. Review collects and assesses challenges. Validation checks encoded requirements. Adoption records a decision to rely on a specified release under an authorised process. Treating one event as proof of all the others makes it difficult for consumers to know what they can trust.

The distinction is particularly important for the Property Pack candidate. Accepted ADR-0066 establishes the 451-item source boundary. Accepted ADR-0067 establishes its modelling approach, and accepted ADR-0075 creates an accelerated Technical Working Group determination sequence. Those decisions do not themselves record that the current candidate has received a favourable determination, industry adoption or government recognition.

## What the proposed lifecycle would add

ADR-0068, first proposed in August 2026, describes a staged human-governed standards process. Its status remains Proposed. It is useful for explaining the intended distinction between stages, but its detailed gates and delegations must not be presented as an operative policy merely because they appear in documentation.

The proposal begins with an evidence record: a source, observation or contribution with provenance and access conditions. An editor's candidate is a reviewable proposal with no official standing. A Working Draft would be adopted by a group as a basis for work without implying final consensus. A Public Review Draft would be sufficiently developed to invite wider scrutiny. A Release Candidate would have comments disposed and implementation evidence being finalised. An OPDA Standard would require ratification and immutable release publication. Later errata, supersession or withdrawal would preserve the historical record while changing its applicable status.

The value of these distinctions is practical. A reviewer can contribute to an unstable candidate without endorsing it. An implementer can experiment with a draft without advertising conformance to an adopted standard. A decision maker can examine readiness evidence without treating a clean test report as proof of consensus. The exact labels and gates become authoritative only through the appropriate adoption of the process.

## Consensus concerns reasons and consequences

A useful review exposes substantive objections rather than counting favourable reactions. Suppose several organisations prefer a short definition, but one participant shows that it merges a legal title with a physical property and makes a required scenario impossible to represent. The counterexample matters because of its consequence, not because of the size of the organisation raising it.

The proposed lifecycle asks for durable issue records, explicit dispositions and recorded dissent. An accepted correction should explain the changed meaning. A deferred issue should retain its owner and reason for deferral. A rejected proposal should retain the evidence and rationale. Silence, meeting attendance and Teams reactions are not interchangeable with reasoned agreement.

Asynchronous participation matters because the people affected by a definition may not attend the same meeting. A discussion becomes reviewable when the proposal, evidence and outcome are available in a persistent record. A transcript can support that record, but an AI summary is not independent proof that a decision was made or an action completed.

## The Property Pack's accelerated determination

ADR-0075 set an initial Technical Working Group determination target at the end of September 2026. Its scoped acceleration allows that initial determination before every domain working group has supplied feedback, with later domain review and controlled change. It does not erase the need to identify the candidate, its evidence and unresolved issues.

Proposed ADR-0090, dated 11 October, records that the September target had elapsed and that an explicit status and sign-off plan were still needed. It distinguishes a requested status update from a declaration of completion and records that the anticipated 8 October update was not delivered. This is a historical status correction, not a new review deadline. The record does not establish a final sign-off.

The same proposal preserves an unresolved 442-versus-451 counting question raised against a combined schema. The accepted source boundary remains 451 until a governed decision changes it. Resolving the discrepancy requires the exact source versions and counting rules: which paths count, how requiredness is interpreted and whether alternative branches or duplicate structures are being counted differently. A numerical difference alone cannot establish that the candidate has lost nine meanings.

## What a determination record needs to make clear

A meaningful determination identifies the exact candidate and scope considered, the reviewers and decision owner, the issues examined, the evidence and the outcome. Conditions, dissent and deferred questions remain part of the result. A later reader should be able to distinguish approval of a limited technical proposition from approval of the entire scheme or a production deployment.

The technical report can demonstrate parsing, shape validation, source coverage and reproducibility. Domain reviewers determine whether the representation makes sense for their use. Cross-boundary questions need the relevant semantic owners. Organisational ratification follows the association's actual authority and recorded delegation. Proposed ADR-0089 explicitly warns that creating an internal technical workspace does not itself grant ratification power.

## Release and continued accountability

Publication should preserve the version examined and link its decision and validation evidence. If a later correction changes a constraint or interpretation, consumers need to know whether their data or implementation is affected. The old release should remain recognisable with an appropriate status rather than silently rewritten.

The standards process is therefore a chain of accountable claims. Evidence supports a proposed meaning; review assesses it; tests check selected consequences; an authorised decision states what has advanced; and release records show what consumers received. Keeping these links distinct makes an accelerated programme more credible, because progress can be stated precisely without pretending that every later decision has already happened.
