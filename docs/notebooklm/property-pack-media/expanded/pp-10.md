# Domain working groups and accountable meaning

For working-group participants, chairs and programme coordinators.

A property transaction connects professions that use overlapping language for different decisions. Lending, conveyancing, surveying and agency may all discuss a property, a party or an assessment, while attaching different conditions to those words. A single centrally imposed definition can hide those distinctions. Completely separate models can make exchange impossible. Accepted ADR-0063 addresses this tension through bounded contexts and explicit agreements at their boundaries.

A bounded context is a place where a meaning is understood consistently enough to be owned and reviewed. It is not simply a department name, a folder or a technical namespace. The documented context boundaries are established inputs for this development phase. Modelling proceeds within them; changing a boundary requires a separate governance decision rather than an incidental edit to a diagram.

## The groups have different concerns

Six property groups cover complementary parts of the work. Finance and Banking considers the mortgage journey, lending decisions, parties, evidence and risk. Conveyancing considers legal transfer, enquiries, searches, exchange and completion. Estate Agency considers marketing, listings, material information, viewings and offers. Surveying and Valuation considers inspection, condition, valuation and professional evidence. Property Data Services considers registries, searches, provenance, currency and reuse. Property Technology considers platforms, workflow, integrations and operational feedback.

The DBT Smart Data group addresses scheme semantics such as participants, trust, consent, authorisation and cross-sector alignment. It is not a seventh property domain or the government department itself. Its terms need a clear semantic home without being used to redefine each property's internal domain concepts.

The Interoperability Working Group draws representatives from the domain and scheme groups. It owns agreements needed at boundaries: the deliberately small common ontology, the context map, qualified mappings and shared conventions. Its purpose is to preserve useful local meaning while enabling exchange, not to redesign every domain from the centre.

## What ownership looks like in practice

Consider an illustrative valuation. A surveyor's assessment is made for a purpose, at a time and under assumptions. A lender may use it as evidence when making a lending decision. The lender's decision is not the same resource as the valuation, even if a system places both in one transaction record. The surveying context explains the assessment; the lending context explains its use in the lending process. A relationship between them must preserve that distinction.

Likewise, a transaction participant's Seller role is not an access-policy role, proof of accreditation or permission to read another person's information. A scheme model can express a justified relationship between participation and permissions, but the domain label alone grants nothing. Accepted ADR-0063 explicitly distinguishes consent, permission and lawful-basis concepts and separates their representation from operational enforcement.

The Property Pack candidate spans several contexts because it is an exchange or delivery profile. Its source items are grouped into work packages to make review manageable. A work package is not automatically a semantic owner. A pack may draw information from several contexts while each resource keeps one declared home and consuming contexts refer to that meaning explicitly.

## Shared meaning needs a narrow common boundary

It can be tempting to move every frequently used term into common. That would recreate the central model the context approach is intended to avoid. A term belongs in the common boundary because several contexts need a stable exchange agreement, not because a shared folder is convenient. Where local meanings remain different, the relationship may be a qualified mapping rather than identity.

A context map shows where meanings originate and how they cross boundaries. A direct object relationship can connect resources owned by different contexts. That does not necessarily assert that two concepts are equivalent. If a mapping is proposed, the affected semantic owners and Interoperability must examine its meaning, scope, direction and evidence. Tools can propose a match; similar labels cannot ratify it.

The eight-category assessment in ADR-0063 helps each group avoid silent gaps. A group can model substantive content itself, reuse an existing shared model, contribute a boundary requirement, or record that a category is not applicable. This avoids creating duplicate vocabularies merely to fill a checklist. The six semantic outputs and the eight categories serve different purposes: outputs describe the package readers use, while categories describe concerns the model must consider.

## Technical assurance is a separate responsibility

The cross-cutting Technology Working Group supplies implementation and technical-assurance evidence. It is not the Property Technology domain group and is not a ninth modelling context. It can test identifiers, provenance, validation, compatibility and release completeness without deciding the business truth of a valuation or legal enquiry. These distinctions make reviews more precise: a failed transformation needs an implementation correction, while an ambiguous definition needs the appropriate domain judgement.

Proposed ADR-0089, dated 11 October 2026, records another distinction: an internal OPDA technical subgroup and wider technology coordination were being clarified. Workspace creation was observed, but the wider remit and adopted Terms of Reference were not established by that research. The proposal must not be retold as an accepted delegation of standards-ratification power. Creating a Team or scheduling a meeting establishes a collaboration surface, not constitutional authority.

## Participation and decisions

Participants contribute examples, challenge omissions and explain consequences. A facilitator translates those contributions into inspectable definitions and diagrams. A chair helps the group address disagreement. AI can accelerate extraction, drafting and checking, but has no vote or authority. Any later normative adoption still requires the appropriate OPDA governance decision; the detailed lifecycle in ADR-0068 remains proposed.

This division of work gives a participant a practical way to act. Identify the meaning affected, its current owner and the consuming contexts. Explain what goes wrong in a concrete case. If the problem crosses a boundary, bring the affected owners together and preserve the disagreement until it has a documented resolution. Accountability becomes visible through those traces, rather than inferred from who spoke most often or which organisation supplied the software.
