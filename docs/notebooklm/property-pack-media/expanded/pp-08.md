# SPDTF development and the separate PDTF evidence lineage

For domain reviewers and implementers.

Four related bodies of work appear in the Development material. Understanding their differences makes the site and the model easier to use. The PDTF schema is the existing Digital Property Pack JSON Schema and its supporting material. The schema-derived ontology is a separate technical derivation from those sources. The Property Pack ontology is a new candidate developed from a closed source scope. SPDTF is the wider Smart Property Data Trust Framework scheme draft being authored collaboratively across industry and stakeholders.

These are neither four names for one product nor four successive approved releases. Their relationships concern evidence, coverage, compatibility and intended development. Their authority does not pass automatically from one to the next.

## What the existing source can establish

The PDTF schema and supporting material describe exchange structures, form overlays, dictionary entries and implementation practices. They are retained as a third-party technical input. They can help explain an existing field, show how an implementation packages information and reveal compatibility requirements. Their inclusion under Development does not mean they were authored through the SPDTF working-group process or constitute an OPDA-endorsed predecessor scheme.

The schema-derived ontology has a different provenance. It is an OPDA-produced technical extraction from those sources, retained as non-normative evidence. It can expose assumptions that are less visible in a nested JSON document. Its diagrams, mappings and validation examples remain tied to that corpus. They are not automatically available features or accepted decisions of the separate Property Pack candidate.

## What the Property Pack candidate changes

Accepted ADR-0066 fixes the new candidate's initial business coverage at the 451 workbook rows marked Required. Accepted ADR-0067 models their meaning through bounded contexts, resources, relationships, controlled terms and constraints. The source scope is closed, while the semantic structure is deliberately reconsidered. A work package groups review effort; a semantic context identifies where a meaning is owned. Neither should be mistaken for a copied JSON branch.

Accepted ADR-0075 places this candidate inside SPDTF development as an accelerated component. The wider future SPDTF ontology is expected to identify its Property Pack part or profile. The candidate is not the entire future framework, and that intended relationship does not make it adopted today. Technical determination, wider domain review, publication and consumer adoption remain distinct events.

## Two different kinds of trace

Source-to-candidate coverage asks how each of the 451 source obligations is represented in the new model. It connects source items to resources, properties, vocabularies, constraints and recorded modelling dispositions. This trace can remain complete even when several source items are consolidated into one modelled relationship or resource.

A semantic crosswalk to the older schema-derived ontology asks a different question: how does an old representation correspond to the new meaning? The retained evidence proves that all 451 required paths occur in the local v3.5 schema. The recorded exact-path lookup found only 34 complete required paths in the older ontology's provenance index. That is not a semantic coverage percentage. Consolidation, different modelling structures and provenance gaps prevent a simple path count from proving equivalence or absence.

A useful crosswalk therefore needs explicit correspondence, evidence and limits. Similar names are insufficient. One source may encode a value directly while another introduces an observation resource; one may distinguish time or authority that the other omits. A declared mapping must explain these differences before a consumer relies on it.

## The practical consequence

Readers can use existing PDTF implementation guidance for the source it describes and inspect the candidate for its own reviewed scope. They should select identifiers, constraints and examples from the correct corpus. SPDTF development can learn from both bodies without inheriting their assumptions as settled standards. Preserving those boundaries makes later compatibility and migration work more trustworthy.

## A framework covers more than a data shape

The retained DPMSG trust-framework explainer describes a prospective exchange in which an authenticated source provider supplies a structured property attribute with provenance, timestamps and other relevant metadata. A receiving application obtains it through an agreed interface. An orchestration service may connect participants and support access control and audit. This is a useful operating explanation; it is not proof that the Property Pack candidate implements every depicted capability.

The example exposes three independent questions. First, what is the attribute and what real-world thing does it concern? That is a semantic question. Second, how is the record encoded and transferred? That is an exchange and interface question. Third, who may obtain it and on what basis may it be relied upon? That involves governance, permissions and operational trust. A scheme needs these layers to work together, while a semantic model must not claim to operate services merely because it contains terms describing them.

Imagine an illustrative source record for a building extension. One system records whether approval evidence was supplied; another records the approval itself; a third stores a conclusion about compliance. Treating all three as the same yes/no field would conceal significant differences. A source-to-model trace should identify what the original item actually establishes. An exchange mapping should then show how that meaning survives transfer. Operational authentication of the sender does not repair a semantic mismatch.

## Compatibility without assumed equivalence

The old and new corpora can coexist as evidence during development. A consumer may need to understand existing JSON payloads and experiment with the candidate's graph structure. That does not require declaring the old source wrong in every respect or the new structure equivalent in every respect. The useful comparison records preserved meaning, changed meaning, unsupported cases and information that must be supplied by context.

Proposed ADR-0091, dated 11 October 2026, calls for a worked ontology-first change process and comparison of modular PDTF schemas with ontology-derived outputs. It leaves approval, compatibility obligations, adapters, coexistence and cutover to the appropriate human process. The proposal does not approve existing pull requests or establish a transition period. A convincing generated example is evidence for that decision, not the decision itself.

This is why stable identifiers and release manifests matter. A reader must be able to say which source, which derived corpus, which candidate and which transformation produced a result. The eventual scheme can then make explicit commitments supported by tested artefacts. Until those commitments are recorded, the Development material remains a place to investigate and improve the draft rather than infer adoption from the presence of a working website.
