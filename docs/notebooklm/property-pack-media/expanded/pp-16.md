# Adoption, interoperability and the responsibilities of data consumers

For organisations evaluating adoption, implementers and people accountable for using property information.

Interoperability means that organisations can exchange information and interpret it sufficiently consistently for a stated purpose. A common file format helps, but it does not settle who may obtain the data, which meaning applies, whether the evidence is current or who takes responsibility for a decision. OPDA's Property Pack candidate contributes a reviewable semantic component to developing SPDTF. It is not, on that basis alone, a released operational scheme or a production adoption commitment.

The retained November 2025 government report, Data Standards for Smart Data, examines property data as one sector case. Its discussion covers sources, standards, access, intermediaries and adoption barriers. The retained property Sandbox Technical Guide describes a pilot and safe non-production environment. These are complementary sources: the report investigates the wider conditions for interoperability, while the guide explains a particular testing arrangement. Neither proves that every proposed SPDTF capability is operational or universally adopted.

## Agree the intended use before assessing conformance

An organisation evaluating a dataset needs a concrete use case. Preparing information for a property listing, assessing a lending application and reviewing a title question draw on overlapping facts, but the required evidence, timing and decision responsibility can differ. A record suitable for one purpose may be incomplete for another even if both organisations call it a property pack.

The consumer therefore needs the version and status of the model or exchange profile, the data provider's scope, the provenance of the supplied facts and the applicable validation rules. Candidate material is useful for evaluation, examples and review. Calling it a candidate preserves the distinction between testing an approach and relying on an authorised release with stated support and change arrangements.

The Property Pack's selected 451 workbook items are a seed boundary, not a promise that every transaction need has been satisfied. The source validation report records many conditional rows and repeated terminal names. A consumer should understand which conditions apply to its case and which source paths a model statement represents. Treating a package as complete because it contains a target number of fields would ignore both applicability and semantic consolidation.

## Separate transport, meaning and permission

Transport compatibility asks whether one system can connect to another and read a response. Semantic compatibility asks whether the receiver interprets the response as intended. Permission asks whether that receiver may obtain and use the data for its purpose. Each can fail while the other two succeed.

The retained Sandbox guide makes this tangible. An onboarded organisation can act as a data provider, receiver or both. Providers register APIs and the associated authorisation arrangements; receivers discover and access permitted APIs. The guide distinguishes transport certificates used for secure connections from signing certificates used for signed material. These controls support authenticated exchange within that pilot's arrangement. They do not establish that every fact in a response is accurate, current or sufficient for a consumer's professional decision.

The guide's prerequisites include particular authorisation-server and gateway arrangements. They describe that sandbox, not a universal requirement for every future SPDTF implementation. An organisation should not infer production certification, universal access rights or a mandatory architecture from successful participation in a pilot. The source labels the environment non-production so that testing and learning can be interpreted in their proper scope.

## Mappings need agreement about loss and difference

The government's data-standards report compares property classifications used by different initiatives, including hierarchical codes and a PDTF enumeration. Similar-looking classifications can have different levels of detail and different purposes. A mapping may be exact for one subset, broader for another and unavailable for some values. A shared label is not sufficient evidence for equivalence.

Suppose, illustratively, one system records a broad property category while another distinguishes several subtypes. Converting the detailed subtype into the broad category may support a particular aggregate use, while converting back cannot recover the original subtype. A receiver needs to know that information was lost. Filling in a more precise value would manufacture information rather than improve interoperability.

Identifiers pose related questions. An identifier may refer to a property, title, transaction, organisation, document or observation. Even where an external identifier is useful, the receiver must understand what it identifies and the scope in which it is reliable. A matching string does not establish that two records describe the same entity in every context. The accepted namespace policy and modelling method address identity discipline, while their implementation status remains separate from the preserved candidate.

## Adoption depends on incentives and operating arrangements

The November 2025 report discusses more than technical standards. Data may be difficult to access, costly to obtain, subject to licence conditions, inconsistently structured or distributed through intermediaries. Organisations also need a reason to invest in changes and confidence about accountability. Publishing a vocabulary does not remove those barriers by itself.

The trust-framework explainer offers a prospective mechanism: a provider exposes structured attributes through an authenticated exchange, with information about source and provenance available to a receiver. That can reduce repeated collection and help a receiver evaluate a claim. Its expected benefits remain dependent on coverage, quality, participation and operating rules. An explanatory account should not be promoted into independent evidence of realised savings or universal data reuse.

An adoption evaluation can therefore exercise a small, representative exchange and observe the whole path: access, identity, parsing, meaning, validation, provenance, exceptions and consumer handling. A fictional case with an outdated statement, a missing conditional answer or a conflicting source is often more revealing than a single uncomplicated success. The findings should name the versions and conditions actually tested.

## Consumers retain responsibility for interpretation and action

A receiver must decide how its application handles uncertainty and conflicting evidence. Does it display a source and observation date? Does it preserve Unknown rather than silently convert it to No? Does it distinguish a missing response from a negative answer? When a provider corrects a statement, can the receiver identify affected uses? These are design and governance questions that a data syntax alone cannot answer.

Validation evidence should travel with its scope: which graph or message, which profile, which version and which checks. A passing result is evidence of those checks, not a guarantee of legal sufficiency, factual truth or fitness for every use. Likewise, a provider's authenticated identity is valuable without making every downstream inference justified.

Proposed ADR-0091 leaves compatibility, coexistence, adapters, deprecation and cutover to explicit migration decisions. Proposed ADR-0090 calls for a recorded Property Pack review status and determination; the elapsed acceleration target does not supply that determination. Adoption claims should consequently distinguish evaluation, pilot participation, implementation of a particular exchange and reliance on an authorised release. That distinction gives organisations a useful basis for deciding what they can test now and what further evidence their intended use requires.
