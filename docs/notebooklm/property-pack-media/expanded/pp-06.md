# How forms, the PDTF schema and the Property Pack relate

For property professionals encountering the Development material for the first time.

A Property Pack is a standardised bundle of property information that accompanies a transaction. The underlying information has a longer history than the current ontology candidate. Property professionals already collect facts and answers through forms, certificates, title information and searches. These sources support different activities and carry their own ownership, licensing, evidence and professional context. A later technical model must preserve those distinctions rather than present all inherited content as newly agreed industry meaning.

The retained Spring 2025 DPMSG Data Standards and Interoperability Report describes Property Pack breadth through BASPI, title information, energy performance certificates, searches and licensed form sources. This establishes a broad information interest. It does not enumerate the current 451-item modelling scope or prescribe an ontology. A report about what belongs in a useful pack and a row-by-row coverage baseline answer different questions.

## From familiar forms to an exchange structure

The existing PDTF schema represents a Digital Property Pack through JSON Schema and supporting overlays, dictionary, glossary and implementation material. A form-oriented structure is useful when software must capture an answer in a particular place and validate a particular exchange. It also preserves signs of its history: repeated question names, nested sections, alternative branches and structures serving several forms.

The later schema-derived ontology was extracted from this technical material. It exposes concepts, relationships, constraints and provenance for inspection. It is a separate draft derivation, not an original part of the JSON Schema and not an approved SPDTF model. Keeping the derivation visible allows reviewers to ask what the inherited structure implies, without assuming that extraction alone established correct meaning.

## Why the new scope contains 451 items

The closed Property Pack seed comes from a retained workbook, PDTF base schema.xlsx. Its Sheet1 contains 1,702 data rows: 451 marked Required and 1,251 Optional. The August 2026 validation found 451 unique required full paths and reconciled all of them structurally to the local PDTF v3.5 schema. That is a strong inventory claim. It is not evidence that every meaning has been settled or that the inventory received final DPMSG approval.

The surrounding correspondence describes an intended core of roughly 450 required fields and consensus still to be built. The validation also identifies important limits: 240 selected rows have no workbook description, 283 have conditional requiredness, and 133 traverse arrays. Required therefore does not mean every leaf must appear in every possible transaction. A condition selecting a branch and the value allowed inside that branch are different things.

Only 100 distinct terminal field names appear across the 451 paths. A name such as yesNo can recur in unrelated questions. The full path identifies the source obligation, but repeated source paths do not demand repeated ontology properties. Conversely, a resource needed to make sense of a relationship may not appear as one primitive workbook row.

## A deliberate new modelling step

Accepted ADR-0066 uses the 451 rows as a closed evidence boundary for a greenfield model. It rejects both repairing the old ontology as the sole approach and creating one RDF predicate per row. The candidate may consolidate repeated fields or introduce the resources, relationships, vocabularies and shapes needed to explain their meaning, provided that their evidence remains traceable.

The inherited schema, optional fields and overlays remain useful for comparison and migration analysis. They do not silently expand the new candidate's business scope. This history explains the present arrangement: preserve the older technical source, inspect its separate derivation, and review a new Property Pack candidate with explicit lineage. It is a schema-to-scheme development journey, not a claim that SPDTF is a renamed, already endorsed predecessor scheme.

## Why digitising a form is only one step

The retained OPDA introductory presentation traces an early practical question to BASPI: could the information needed by the buyer and seller be collected digitally? Its account says the investigation widened to the rest of the pack, including certificate, title and search information. This is the programme's own account of its development. It explains why a useful pack reaches across several evidence producers rather than treating one form as the entire property record.

A completed document can contain useful information without making that information readily reusable by software. An illustrative seller's answer may be buried in a PDF, accompanied by a date, a qualification and an attachment. Extracting only the answer text loses the relationship to the question and the evidence. Capturing it as structured data helps, but the meaning still depends on the question, the person answering and the circumstances. Modelling those distinctions is different from reproducing the form's page layout.

The government's retained Data Standards for Smart Data research describes the same difficulty from a sector perspective. It distinguishes data holders, transaction intermediaries and software suppliers, and notes that source digitisation and common exchange standards must develop together. A technically sound exchange format cannot supply information that the holder has not made available in a usable form. Equally, digitising a source does not ensure that every receiving system interprets its fields consistently.

## Historical rules remain historical evidence

The repository's NTS source note explicitly marks the retained Sales and Lettings material-information guidance as withdrawn, with status recorded in October 2025. It also records that historical schema overlays implement those sources. Those retained rules are useful for explaining why a field or constraint entered an earlier technical package. They are not an up-to-date statement of the law or permission to apply an old overlay to a new release without review.

Form editions matter for the same reason. A label such as TA6 identifies a family of documents, not an eternal unchanging set of questions. Source identity needs the edition, date, relevant rights and exact field or passage. A modeller comparing a workbook to an inherited schema must therefore establish which versions are being compared before claiming that a missing question is an omission.

This gives the historical material a productive role. It supports coverage checks, examples, licensing questions and migration analysis. Its constraints can be retained, challenged or left unresolved with reasons. A new model remains faithful to its evidence when it records those dispositions; fidelity does not require copying every source arrangement or treating all inherited rules as current obligations.
