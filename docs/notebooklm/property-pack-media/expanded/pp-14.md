# Working together through discussion, meetings and durable records

For working-group participants, facilitators and people supporting collaboration.

Working together requires a clear home for each activity. OPDA's participant guidance uses Teams for group discussion, a separate SharePoint intake boundary for organisation source material, and the knowledge base for reading and page-specific feedback. Meetings bring questions together; reviewed records preserve the outcome. Email carries invitations and support and can provide a screened evidence fallback. These channels serve related purposes, but access to one does not establish access to all of them or authority to determine a standard.

Accepted ADR-0070 defines the workspace pattern. Accepted ADR-0085, updated 22 September 2026, records independent approval for each property domain and the follow-up that prepares access and a domain-specific invitation. The documented provisioning table distinguishes implemented workspaces from planned ones. The pattern is therefore a common design and an operating arrangement for provisioned groups, not proof that every listed group has an identical live service today.

## Participation starts with an explicit scope

Requesting interest in several domains does not grant membership in all of them. Under ADR-0085, staff review each domain separately. A person approved for Finance and Banking is not thereby approved for Conveyancing. An acknowledgement confirms a submission; an approval invitation follows the relevant human decision. Neither grants standards decision authority beyond the applicable governance arrangements.

This separation also matters when an organisation's systems block external Microsoft access. Website eligibility, Microsoft provisioning and email delivery have different dependencies. A participant should not interpret a delayed Microsoft invitation as proof that a website approval was reversed. Conversely, receiving a forwarded invitation does not authorise someone else to use it. Invitations bind to an intended identity and should not be circulated as general group links.

The proposed non-Microsoft participation approach in ADR-0092 aims to prevent corporate tenant restrictions from excluding expertise. Its recorded requirement should not be described as universally verified access. A facilitator needs to establish a usable authorised route for the particular participant and preserve the same source-handling boundaries through any fallback.

## Put a question where the group can follow it

The participant guide distinguishes general orientation, common-topic coordination and stable topic discussions. Channel names organise work; they are not a substitute for permissions. The separate source-intake site remains the place for controlled organisation submissions, even when a discussion is happening in a domain channel.

Before starting a new thread, search for the existing question. Reply in that thread when the point belongs to the same issue. A new thread should have a clear subject and one main question, with a link or reference to the affected candidate, definition or evidence. This reduces the risk that the reasoning is split across several conversations that reach apparently different conclusions.

Uploading a file does not require a new announcement thread merely to say that a file exists. Its source index can explain what was submitted. A discussion becomes useful when it asks something specific about interpretation, scope or a proposed change. “Does this source's completion date mean the same event as the candidate's date?” gives the group a question to resolve. It should identify the relevant versions and use fictional examples where transaction detail is needed.

Participants should distinguish an observed fact, their organisation's practice, a proposal and a personal opinion. All can contribute, but they carry different weight. An established internal process may reveal a requirement without being suitable as an industry-wide rule. Conflicts of interest and competition-sensitive topics also need attention: customer strategies, commercially sensitive pricing or market allocation do not become appropriate group material because the discussion concerns data standards.

## Prepare a meeting around decisions people can understand

The current guide asks for the agenda, relevant candidate version, evidence and questions in advance. Participants can then comment before the meeting and identify missing examples. This helps people who cannot attend and makes the meeting less dependent on whoever speaks first or understands technical notation fastest.

A productive modelling discussion can use ordinary language. What does this term mean? Which object does this fact describe? Is this relationship pointing in the right direction? What happens when the evidence changes? Which group owns the meaning, and which other group relies on it? These are domain questions. The facilitator can translate the result into a technical proposal without making live RDF editing the price of participation.

Consider a meeting about two organisations' different uses of “verified.” One might mean a document was checked; another might mean a claim passed a particular verification method. The group should inspect each definition and a contrasting example before deciding whether the terms are shared, related or distinct. The meeting record should preserve the distinction and the outstanding question, rather than replace both with a single apparently agreeable label.

## Recording a discussion does not settle its meaning

Recording and transcription depend on the stated arrangements for notice, consent, access and retention. Participants should know how the record will be used. A transcript should preserve enough date, speaker and context information for a reviewer to understand the exchange. Restricted discussion should not be copied into public documentation simply because transcription made it easy to reuse.

An automated transcript can mishear a term, omit a qualification or merge speakers. Even an accurate transcript captures discussion, including tentative suggestions and disagreement. It is evidence for a reviewed record; it is not a list of ratified decisions. A facilitator should distinguish an agreed action from an accepted definition, an open issue from a rejected option, and a request for evidence from proof that the evidence exists.

After a meeting, the guide calls for permitted notes and actions linked to the canonical question, with an opportunity for asynchronous feedback. Where a deadline is stated, it should be explicit. Attendance, silence and reactions should not be treated as consensus. Proposed ADR-0068 describes lifecycle and review arrangements, but its status does not make every proposed threshold operative.

## Make continuity possible between meetings

The next participant should be able to find the candidate version, the question, the relevant source, the discussion and the recorded disposition without reconstructing a private conversation. This does not require copying every message into a new register. It requires preserving links between the existing records and keeping a clear distinction between evidence, proposal, action and determination.

For example, a meeting may agree that a source owner will supply a fictional exception case. That action can be completed when the case arrives, while the modelling question remains open until review. A later candidate may incorporate a change, but that still does not establish a production release. Each state answers a different practical question: what can participants access, what has been received, what has been decided and what can consumers rely on?
