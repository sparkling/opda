# Identity, agents and roles across property systems

For domain specialists, data modellers and integration teams.

Two records can share an address without describing the same thing. One may describe a building, another a title and another a transaction involving it. Similarly, “buyer” can describe a person's role in one transaction without defining what that person permanently is. Identifiers and familiar labels help locate possible connections, but they do not settle those distinctions.

The accepted OPDA modelling method addresses two connected questions. ODR0041 distinguishes kinds, roles and phases. ODR0059 describes criterion-based identity across independently governed contexts. The retained DBT data-standards report adds practical property examples showing why those distinctions matter. The method records are accepted design rules with implementation obligations; they are not evidence that an automatic matching service has been delivered.

## A property identifier is useful within a scope

The DBT report explains that the Unique Property Reference Number supports linking information about addressable locations across datasets. It gives energy certificates and links to energy-meter identifiers as useful examples. This makes the UPRN an important integration aid, but the report also records limits.

A title does not necessarily correspond one-to-one with an addressable location: one title can cover multiple UPRNs. A house converted into flats can also change the addressable locations and the status of associated identifiers. These are source-reported examples, not a freshly verified inventory of identifier services. They show why an identifier must be understood in relation to the thing and lifecycle it identifies.

Consider an illustrative lender record for a security, a conveyancer record for a title and a surveyor record for a building. The records may all mention the same postal address. Joining them can be helpful, but merging their subjects would lose the distinction between the physical object, legal interests and lending arrangement. A relationship can preserve that connection without declaring the things identical.

## People persist while roles change

In an illustrative chain, one person sells an existing home and buys another. The person does not become two individuals because two systems label them “seller” and “buyer”. Nor does one role erase the other. The roles depend on their relationships to different transactions and can apply simultaneously.

ODR0041 describes a role as dependent on relational context: its bearer can stop playing it without ceasing to exist. The method distinguishes a class-level declaration of what may bear a role from an instance-level relationship recording actual participation. A person being eligible to buy a property does not establish that they are the buyer in a particular transaction.

That distinction also applies to organisations and software. An organisation may provide one dataset and receive another. Its application is a piece of software acting within an operating arrangement, not the organisation itself. A registered role or certificate may help establish what an application is allowed to do, but it does not make every action by that software an authorised action in every context.

## Kinds, subkinds and phases

A kind supplies a criterion for identifying its instances. A genuine subkind retains that criterion while adding a permanent restriction. A role depends on a relationship. A phase describes change over time without necessarily creating a new individual. These distinctions prevent a convenient database table or interface flag from determining ontology structure by accident.

For example, the method's discussion of a dwelling passing through lifecycle states explains why a change of state need not imply replacement of the dwelling. The detailed state-transition record belongs to the temporal model. A class-level phase annotation cannot itself say when a particular transition happened.

The method therefore asks authors to analyse identity before choosing subclass inheritance, role or phase annotations. A boolean flag can be evidence worth investigating, but it is not sufficient proof that the model needs a meaningful role class. The interpretation must be supported by domain facts and relationships.

## Similarity finds candidates; evidence determines the relation

ODR0059 starts by distinguishing classes from controlled classification concepts. A country or status vocabulary belongs to scheme alignment rather than a process for merging kinds. For class meanings, the method then considers category compatibility, an admissible identity criterion and boundary or coherence conflicts.

A shared database key is useful evidence only if the properties behind it track the relevant identity criterion. A high similarity score cannot substitute for that reasoning. Two definitions may use nearly identical words while applying to different populations or times. Conversely, different labels can describe a justified common meaning.

The method allows several outcomes. A mapping may support exact correspondence, broader or narrower correspondence, a close match, an association, or a recorded separation. These outcomes express different claims. Confidence describes evidence quality; it does not select a stronger relationship merely by crossing a numerical threshold.

An associated title and building are a straightforward teaching example of connection without interchangeability. The example does not prescribe a particular mapping predicate for the production model; that requires the actual definitions and relation being reviewed. The lesson is to state what the evidence warrants before choosing the encoding.

## Why the decision must remain repairable

An equivalence decision has consequences beyond the pair first considered. ODR0059 calls for human reinspection of exact-match chains and for versioned evidence. If new information falsifies an identity criterion, the mapping must be retracted through an explicit change rather than silently removed.

The method also recognises the cost of excessive separation. A previously denied match may become supportable after a source revision, a new context or improved identity evidence. Reopening those decisions prevents caution from hardening into permanent fragmentation. Useful integration depends on both avoiding false merges and discovering justified connections.

Within one governed context, accidental system boundaries should be reconciled directly. Across genuinely independent contexts, qualified mappings preserve the different responsibilities. Promotion of a shared kind needs evidence and a named stewardship authority; repeated use of the same label is insufficient.

For a practitioner, the central questions are concrete: what thing does this record identify; which relationship makes this party a participant; what changes while identity persists; and what evidence supports the link to another system? Answering them makes identifiers more useful and prevents a convenient join from becoming an unintended claim about the world.
